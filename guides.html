Practical guides for your digital life.

Short, independent guides to help you choose online services more carefully, understand common plan labels and protect your files and information.

1. How to choose an AI tool

Start with the task, not the brand name. A tool that is excellent at writing may not be the right choice for image editing, voice transcription or research.

Define the job

  • Writing and brainstorming: check whether the service supports your language, document length and preferred writing style.
  • Research: look for source links, dates and a way to verify important claims independently.
  • Image or video creation: check output limits, watermark rules, commercial-use terms and whether generated files can be downloaded.
  • Voice and transcription: check supported languages, audio duration limits and how recordings are stored.

Check before signing up

  • Open the provider's official website rather than an imitation or sponsored lookalike.
  • Read the privacy policy and avoid pasting passwords, identity documents, private customer data or confidential work into an AI prompt.
  • Check the current free limits, account requirements, age restrictions and cancellation terms.
  • Verify important answers using reliable primary sources. AI output can be incomplete or incorrect.
A useful AI tool is one that solves your actual task, explains its limits and gives you enough control over your data and output.

2. Use online file tools more safely

Before choosing a PDF, image, office or conversion utility, find out where the file is processed. A browser-based interface does not automatically mean every operation stays on your device.

Before selecting a file

  • Read the tool's explanation to see whether processing happens locally or on a provider's server.
  • Do not use public or shared computers for private documents unless you can safely remove the files and downloads afterwards.
  • Avoid uploading bank statements, identity documents, medical records, passwords or confidential business files to an unfamiliar service.
  • Keep an untouched copy of important files before converting, compressing, merging or editing them.

After processing

  • Open the output and check pages, formatting, image quality and filenames before sharing it.
  • Delete uploaded files from the provider if it offers a deletion control, and review its retention policy.
  • Use HTTPS and check that the domain is the provider's genuine website.
If you cannot tell where a file goes or how long it is stored, do not upload sensitive material.

3. Understand Free, Freemium and Paid

These labels are a starting point, not a promise that every feature is free or that a price will stay the same.

  • Free: the provider offers the described service without a required payment, although limits, optional purchases or account requirements may still apply.
  • Freemium: some features are available at no cost, while higher limits or additional features may require payment.
  • Paid: access to the relevant service or plan generally requires payment.

Before relying on a service, check its current pricing page for usage caps, trial periods, renewal dates, taxes, cancellation rules and regional availability. ORBIT's labels are general guidance and may not capture every plan condition.
